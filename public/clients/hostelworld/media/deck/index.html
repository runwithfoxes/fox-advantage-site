Your app and your site are the bottle.

Dove invests in the bottle because the bottle is where the promise is kept. Hostelworld does not have a bottle. It has an app and a website. That is the product, the packaging and the shelf, all at once. Every promise the marketing makes gets paid off, or lost, right there.

Search captures demand. It does not create it.

This is essential. It is the till at the front of the shop, and it has to work. But it only reaches people who are already looking. Spend everything here and you are not bringing customers in, you are collecting the ones who arrived on their own. It is a cold call from a brand nobody has heard of, and it only pays once the work above it is done.

AI runs through all of it.

A thinking partner

Interrogating a plan, finding the gaps in your own process, arguing with your assumptions before the market does. This is the use most teams never get to.

Speed of execution

Making the work faster to produce, so more of the budget goes into ideas and reach and less into production. Speed is the obvious use. It is not the biggest one.

Repeatable systems

Turning the things you do every week into workflows that run the same way every time, so the team is freed up for the work that only humans can do.

What I would ask of a marketing team today
Be curious. Think critically. Keep pushing at the edge of what it can do, because the edge moves every month.

Questions one to three.
Question 01
Are we building against the real entry points?
Growth that adds bookings is not the same as growth that adds value.
  • 8x the margin. Social members vs non-social, €35 vs €4. Engaged members 11x, at €46.
  • New social members outnumber non-social every quarter, 0.83m vs 0.45m. The mix is improving. It needs accelerating.
  • 6 entry points identified. Find My People and Cheap Bed validating first, the rest through H2.
→ Customer and Cohort Intelligence Engine
Question 02
Too few know us. Fewer know what we stand for.
Recognition is not meaning. No single idea, and no distinctive assets yet.
  • UK test, quality execution alone: +40% click-through.
  • Tracksuit: 12% aware against a 34% category in the UK. Among those who are aware, social association is already above 50%.
→ Brand Positioning and Creative Engine
Question 03
Are we investing in demand tomorrow, or only chasing today’s booking?
Search only reaches people who are already ready to buy.
  • Search reaches roughly 5% of the category that is in market today.
  • EU test: +12% new customers, with 1.5 to 2.5x payback inside the year.
  • 660k+ Instagram brand tags, 2.5m+ followers. A creator-led, long-term compounding play.
→ Demand Creation Engine + Unmissable Social Engine
Questions four and five.
Question 04
Is buying us easy? The first time, and every time after?
Friction anywhere between wanting to book and booking again undoes the investment that got them there in the first place.
  • At the click, for new customers. Route straight to the app, not to mobile web. Correctly routed clicks already convert at 3.9% against 2.7% on web.
  • After the fact, for existing customers. CRM migrates past web bookers onto the app and keeps the brand in mind between trips. Win-back of roughly 940k lapsed customers is proven. Second booking and home-city events are piloting.
→ CRM, Lifecycle and Web-to-App Engine
Question 05
How do we know it works before trading shows it?
We need a system that judges results over the right timeframe, not just the click.
  • Meta self-reported 87% incremental. The independent check said 67%.
  • Effects take time to land. Roughly a 3-week onset and 6 to 10 weeks of carryover. Day-to-day attribution misses this, and cannot referee whether spend created new demand.
  • Build it properly. Tracksuit reads the market moving. Live incrementality testing is the referee. Channel attribution stays in its lane, sharpened, but not the judge.
→ Measurement and Decisioning Engine
