Station 1 · The owner's ask
High-level one-pager
Owner: VP, Airline Retailing  ·  Campaign: Modular retailing, “Built to open”  ·  For: Product Marketing

Core positioning — the angle, one line

Sabre’s retailing platform is modular: airlines adopt what they need, when they need it, without ripping out what they already run. Built to open, not a big-bang rebuild, against the all-or-nothing monolith incumbents.

Target audience — who, at altitude

Airline commercial and retailing leaders (VP Retailing, Head of Offer & Order, Chief Commercial) at mid-to-large carriers weighing offer-and-order transformation. Secondary: their IT and architecture leads, who carry the fear of big-bang risk and vendor lock-in.

Key elements — what the campaign includes

  • Hero thought-leadership piece
  • Webinar with a launch customer
  • Three-part email sequence
  • Paid social and display (the OPEN / PLATFORM ad routes)
  • One sales-enablement sheet, all hung off a single proof point

Desired outcomes — success, with targets

  • [XX] qualified airline conversations into pipeline
  • [XX] webinar registrations, [XX]% from target carriers
  • Influence [€X]m of retailing pipeline this half

Timeline

Built around an industry moment, live ahead of [IATA / airline retailing event, date]. Roughly a [6–8 week] runway from brief to launch.

Translations

English core. Localise the email and landing copy for [LATAM and APAC] if we target carriers there. Ad creative stays English.

Amplification strategy

Owned (Sabre channels plus sales outreach to named accounts), paid (LinkedIn to airline commercial titles), earned (PR around the customer story), partner and ambassador push at the event.

Station 2 · The marketing craft
The brief
Owner: Product Marketing  ·  Reviewed against April Dunford positioning  ·  For: Campaign Manager

1. Persona story — who we’re really talking to

Maya, VP of Retailing at a mid-size carrier. She owns ancillary and offer revenue and she is under board pressure to modernise. She has seen a multi-year platform rebuild stall at another airline and she will not bet the airline on a freeze-and-replace. Her quiet fear is not the technology, it is the cutover: the day the new system goes live and bookings stop. She wants to show progress this year, not in three. The person who can block her is her own architecture lead, who has been burned by vendor lock-in before.

2. Positioning — Dunford frame, so every line is defensible

  • Competitive alternatives: rip-and-replace monolith platforms; staying on legacy PSS with bolt-ons; building in-house.
  • Unique attributes: modular architecture adopted component by component; runs alongside the existing stack; open APIs; no big-bang cutover.
  • Value those enable: transformation without the big-bang risk; each module earns revenue on its own; keep what already works; the airline controls the pace.
  • Customers who care most: mid-to-large carriers mid-transformation who cannot afford a system freeze.
  • Category / frame of reference: modern airline retailing (offer and order), framed as modular, not monolith.

3. Messaging framework

North star: Built to open. Modernise your retailing one module at a time, without betting the airline.

PillarThe messageProof (to source / verify)
No big-bang risk Adopt module by module, running alongside what you already have. No cutover day. [Named carrier went live module-by-module, no freeze — verify]
Revenue sooner Each module stands on its own, so value lands in months, not years. [Time-to-value figure — verify against a real deployment]
Open by design Open APIs, your data, built to standards. No lock-in. [IATA Offer & Order / NDC alignment — confirm exact standards]

4. Proof points grounded in data — the spine of credibility

  • One named launch customer with a real module-by-module story. [Owner to confirm who is referenceable.]
  • One hard adoption or revenue metric from that deployment. [Source from the account team.]
  • One piece of third-party validation, analyst note or standards body. [Verify before use.]

5. Register / tone

Peer-to-peer, calm, expert. We are de-risking a scary decision, not hyping a product. No big-bang language ourselves. Sabre’s own lexicon fits: “no big-bang risk, no vendor lock-in,” “built to open.”
