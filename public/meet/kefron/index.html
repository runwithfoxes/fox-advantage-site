Every finance team we speak to has the same three questions about e-invoicing. Is it mandatory for us yet, which format, and what happens to the invoices that still arrive as PDFs and paper. The third one decides whether automation works at all. Capture that needs a template per supplier breaks the first time a supplier changes their layout. Capture that reads the invoice, validates it with a human check where it matters, and routes it for approval does not break. That is the standard we hold Kefron AP to, and it is the question worth asking of any system you are looking at.

We still key invoices by hand
Manual invoice processing tends to show up as late approvals, month end that depends on one or two people, and a team that cannot take leave without worrying. The usual fix is capture that reads and validates each invoice and routes it for approval without a template per supplier. The best next step is a conversation with one of Kefron's specialists about how your invoices arrive and where they get stuck.

Our records are on paper
Paper records are slow to find, hard to audit and easy to lose track of, especially once they sit in more than one building. The question is usually which records need scanning, which need secure physical storage, and what the retrieval time has to be. One of Kefron's specialists can walk through that with you and map out what an end to end approach would look like.

We are retiring old devices
Retiring laptops and phones is mostly a data question. Every device needs its data destroyed in a way you can prove later, and the hardware handled sustainably. That takes a certified process with a chain of custody from collection to disposal. One of Kefron's specialists can talk you through what that looks like for the volume you have.
