// bellinter house, co. meath

Worth the drive

Bellinter House is a Georgian country house hotel and spa on the Boyne, half an hour from Dublin. We keep sending people there, so here it is written down.

\ built around 1750
\ 12 acres

// the house

Older than nearly everything around it

Bellinter was built around 1750 by Richard Castle, the architect behind half the big houses in Ireland, for a man called John Preston. Preston's grandson became the first Lord Tara, which tells you the sort of company the place kept.

More than two hundred and seventy years on, it is still standing on the south bank of the Boyne, within sight of the Hill of Tara, doing roughly what it was built to do: feeding people and putting them up for the night.

"A house built to entertain in 1750, still entertaining."

// the setting

Twelve acres on the Boyne

The house sits in twelve acres of Meath countryside, five minutes off the M3 and about half an hour from both Dublin city and the airport. Six miles from Navan if you need a town. Close enough to be easy, far enough to feel like you've left.

// the bathhouse

A ten-metre pool to do nothing in

The spa is called the Bathhouse, and for a lot of people it is the whole reason to come. There's a thermal suite with a ten-metre infinity pool, hot tubs, saunas and a cold plunge for the brave, then a row of heated loungers for after.

Treatments use VOYA, the Irish seaweed-skincare lot. You can lose an afternoon in there without much trouble.

// where you sleep

Rooms in the house, and beyond it

You can stay in the main Georgian house itself, in the suites looking over the Boyne valley, or out in the grounds in the old Schoolhouse and Stables. Different rooms for different moods and budgets.

// the table

Preston's, and dogs at the door

Dinner is in Preston's, named for the family that built the place, and the kitchen leans on what's grown and reared nearby, which in Meath is no hardship.

One more thing that makes it easy to like: they welcome dogs, and have a whole setup for them. The dog comes too.
