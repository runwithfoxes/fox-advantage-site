# Gimnasio Zorro

Fictional. Built for the UCD x IE week, September 2026. Every person in it is made up.

Gimnasio Zorro is a neighbourhood gym in Chamberí, Madrid. One floor of weights and machines, a
studio for classes, open 6am to 11pm. About 900 members. The owner is Marta Ferrer. She opened it
nine years ago, still works the desk most mornings, and knows a lot of members by name. Three staff
on the floor, two class instructors, nobody in an office.

What the gym is for, in Marta's words: this is the gym you actually keep going to. Not the cheapest
in Madrid, not the biggest. The one where someone notices when you have not been in.

That last line is the positioning, and it is the problem too. Marta knows that a member who stops
coming for a few weeks tends not to renew. She knows it from nine years of watching it happen. She
does not have the hours to go through 900 members every Monday, work out who is drifting, and write
each of them a proper note. So the thing the gym is for is the thing the gym cannot do.

The members. Roughly a quarter joined to lose weight, a fifth to build muscle, a fifth for general
fitness. The rest are training for something (a half marathon, a wedding, a ski trip), managing
stress and sleep, getting stronger as they get older, or coming back after an injury. Each member
told the gym their goal when they joined and it is on their record. Some records carry notes from
the desk: away in August, knee injury, comes once a fortnight and that is fine.

The plans. Monthly, annual, off-peak mornings, student. The plan matters less than the pattern.
An off-peak member who came every morning and has stopped is a bigger loss than an annual member
who always came once a fortnight.

What the agent does for Marta. Every Monday it reads the door log and the member records, finds the
members who are drifting from their own pattern, writes each one a short personal note in Marta's
voice off what they said they wanted, and puts the flag and the draft on the member's record for
Marta to approve. Two weeks later it looks again and tells her how many came back. It never sends
anything itself. It never chases someone whose record says injured or away. When a reply says
something the agent cannot judge, it asks Marta.
