What I'd recommend

What we would build for Supply-IT

This comes from our conversation. Supply-IT has spent fifteen years as a trade supplier, selling phone and computer parts to repair shops across Ireland, and it has done that well on next-day delivery and word of mouth, with almost no marketing. You are now opening to the public for the first time, with a shop in Cork and a new consumer website selling finished goods. That is a different job. A trade customer knows exactly what they want and comes to you for it. A member of the public arrives cold, decides in a few seconds whether to trust the brand, and needs to be guided to the right product. The two pieces of work below are what that shift needs, in the order we would do them.

First · the thinking and the look

Positioning, messaging and a brand identity

We start by agreeing how the new consumer business is described and sold, because everything after it depends on this. That means deciding who it is for, what it does that the alternatives do not, and how you want it to feel the moment someone lands on it. You already have the raw material. You deliver anywhere on the island next day, you carry the parts nobody else bothers to stock, and you can use AI on the site to tell a customer exactly which charger or part they need from a photo. Very few consumer businesses in this category can say any of that. The job is to turn it into a clear position and a short set of messages that everything then uses.

Once the position is agreed, we design the identity: the name, the logo, the colours, the type and the overall look of the consumer brand. For this I bring in a senior creative team I have worked with for years, including on brand work at the National Lottery, so you get genuinely high-end creative here rather than a template. As we discussed on the call, this is the part that decides whether a first-time visitor trusts you enough to buy. When a brand is new and competing on price, looking established and premium is what earns that trust.

Then · the build

The website

Building a consumer website has usually meant a designer, a developer and someone to manage both, over several months. We do that work with AI instead, which will make sense to you, because you already use AI on your own site and build things in-house. It starts from the positioning and identity above, so the site is built in the new brand from the start, rather than designed once and then handed to a developer to reproduce.

Once a site is built this way, you own it and you can change it. Adding a page, a seasonal offer or a new product range takes minutes, and your own team can do it by typing what they want in plain English. That matters for you specifically. You told me the lead times on stock are long and the year runs to a calendar, from spring to back-to-school to Christmas. A site the team can update themselves keeps up with that without waiting on anyone.

Why this works

How we keep this to €12,000

Work like this, done the traditional way, usually runs to tens of thousands and takes months. A positioning and identity project alone can be forty to sixty thousand euros through an agency, before a website is even started. We can do both, properly, for twelve thousand, and there are a few specific reasons why.

The first is that most of the cost and time in agency work does not go on the work itself. It goes on presentations, on selling ideas back and forth, on layers of account management, and on reworking things two and three times because decisions were slow or unclear. Take that out and what is left is the actual thinking and building, which is the part that matters.

The second is that we use AI to prototype quickly. Rather than describe an idea in a document and wait weeks to see it, we build a working version fast, look at it, and change it. You react to something real instead of a description, which removes most of the back and forth that usually slows a project down and runs the bill up.

The third is discipline on both sides. You make decisions quickly and clearly, and we move fast without padding the process. When those hold, we can do genuinely high-quality work at speed and get the whole thing done end to end in about a month. If decisions stall or the scope keeps growing, that is what pushes cost and time up in any project. Keeping it tight is how the number stays where it is.
