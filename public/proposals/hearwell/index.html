What I'd recommend

A marketing plan first, then the systems that run it

This comes from our conversation. You are opening five clinics and thirty-one satellite locations inside GP medical centres, and you launch at the end of October or in November. You already have the positioning, the tagline, four audiences with a message for each, and a set of ground rules covering how your clinical claims and your own naming can be used. We would build on that rather than start again. What is missing is the plan that turns it into a market, and the systems to execute that plan at the volume thirty-six locations will demand. So the first half of this is marketing strategy and planning, done with you. The second half is building what the plan calls for, in your name, handed to your team.

One thing shapes everything else. At one hundred and ten euro a month, you recover the cost of winning a patient over years. The traditional model takes five and a half to six and a half thousand on day one. So wherever you and an incumbent are bidding for the same person, they can pay more than you and still come out ahead. That is why the GP satellites matter as much as they do, and it is why the plan has to be built on referral and reputation rather than on buying attention.

Part one · the thinking

The marketing strategy and plan

Before anything is built, we decide how HearWell Health goes to market. That means a practical strategy and execution plan rather than a positioning document: which audiences to reach and in what order, how GP referrals actually get generated, where the money goes and where it does not, and the creative and messaging that carries it. I work as your marketing lead while we do it, so it gets decided with you rather than handed to you.

You told me you are stuck on the call to action. It is a hard line to write, because it has to carry the whole argument in a few words, and it cannot really be settled before the strategy is. We settle it as part of this work.

What the plan decides
  • How you go to marketThe audiences, the channels and the sequence, built around a model where acquisition has to be cheap.
  • How the GP engine is actually fedThirty-one of your locations sit inside their premises, so referrals are how the rollout works. The plan treats clinicians as an audience in their own right, with their own argument and their own material.
  • Where the budget goesAllocation across channels and across the launch window, including what we would not spend on.
  • The call to action, settledThe line that replaces the free hearing test every competitor runs, and the argument sitting underneath it.
  • How we measure itEnquiries, assessments booked and the cost of acquiring a patient, tracked against a model where the money comes back monthly.

Part one · the thinking

Brand strategy, positioning and guidelines

Inside the plan we complete the brand work. You already have the healthy-ageing reframe, hEARsphere™ as the reason to choose you, the Signature Programme as the offer, and a set of rules covering how your clinical evidence and your own naming may be used. What we build on top of that is the layer that decides how all of it looks, sounds and behaves in public, and then makes it repeatable across thirty-six locations and everyone who will be writing for you.

That matters more here than in most businesses, because your category is governed in practice even where it is not in law. If a claim goes out without its qualification, or the programme is described with the wrong word, the problem is a regulatory one rather than a cosmetic one. So the guidelines are written as rules that get checked on every asset rather than as a document people are asked to remember. That is what the Brand Guardian below does.

What this covers
  • The brand strategyWhat HearWell Health stands for, who it is for, and what it will not do, written so it still holds when other people are executing it.
  • Positioning, finishedYour existing line taken from a statement into an argument that works against Specsavers, against the incumbents, and against doing nothing for another seven years.
  • Guidelines that can be enforcedType, colour, imagery, tone, naming and claims, written as rules a person or an agent can be held to.
  • The clinical claim rulebookYour own rules, encoded: the ACHIEVE finding never travelling without its prespecified high-risk older subgroup qualification, hEARsphere™ keeping its capital EAR, the Signature Programme never being called anything else in front of a patient, and a minimum text contrast that a fifty-to-seventy audience can actually read.
One decision worth making early
You told me the logo is a Canva draft and not where you want it to land. You also have a lease agreed in Dublin 4 and a fit-out under way. Changing a web page is cheap and changing signage is not, so the identity decision wants to be made before the signage is ordered rather than after. It is a short conversation and worth having in the first week.

The plan only counts for what gets executed, so the second half of the work is building the systems that run it, at the standard twenty years in brand sets and at the speed a business opening in October needs. Your team approves what goes out.

I have a range of these already built and proven. Which ones HearWell Health needs is a decision that falls out of the strategy and plan, not something to fix now. When I do the marketing strategy, I work out how it gets executed, and that is what tells me which systems are worth building for you.

Brand Guardian

Checks every asset against your own rulebook before it goes out: the ACHIEVE qualification, hEARsphere™ naming, the Signature Programme wording, the imagery rules and the contrast floor. A group of freelancers cannot give you this.

Website Builder

Lifecycle Agent

Campaign Manager
