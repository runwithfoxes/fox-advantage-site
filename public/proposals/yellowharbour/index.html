What I'd recommend

What we would build for Yellow Harbour

This comes from our conversation. Yellow Harbour has doubled in the last year with very little marketing behind it, you have around fifty clients and you are confident of the capacity for a hundred, so the job now is the sales pipeline. Your market is well defined: the SME charity with an income between €500,000 and €1 million, where the CEO holds the grants, and there are thousands of charities on the public register to work through. Until now that pipeline has been built by hand: interns working through the register, finding the CEO, finding an email address, adding them to the newsletter. It worked, and it is slow. And because a charity's decision goes through its volunteer board, it takes around three months from first conversation to a signed client, so the pipeline has to be kept full continuously rather than in bursts. You are also five or six months into using AI agents on the grant-writing side, so you have already seen what this looks like when it is done properly. Below is what I would recommend we start with, configured for Yellow Harbour and handed to your team.

First · the thinking

Positioning and messaging

I said on our call that I do not think Yellow Harbour has a positioning problem, and I meant it. You are clear about what you do, who it is for, and why it is worth paying for. What is missing is only that none of it is written down as a working set of messages that other work can be built on.

So we would start with a two-hour interview with you and David, and convert it into a messaging framework: the positioning, the tone of voice, and the insights about your customers, the busy CEO who knows the grants are not getting the attention they deserve. Every message the Outbound Agent writes is grounded in that framework, which is what keeps the quality high when the volume goes up. Later, the same framework serves the newsletter and the website, so everything says the same thing.

What this covers
  • How Yellow Harbour is describedA short, agreed statement of what you do and who it is for, written so that a charity CEO can repeat it accurately to their board.
  • Who we write toThe SME charity with an income between €500,000 and €1 million, and the CEO who holds the grants.
  • The messages themselvesWritten once, then used by the outreach first and, later, by the newsletter and the website, so everything says the same thing.

Later · the rest of the team

The other agents, when you are ready

The Outbound Agent is one member of a team. Each of the agents below is built the same way: configured for Yellow Harbour, grounded in the same messaging framework, and handed over to your team. Each one is a separate, later decision, and we would only add one once the Outbound Agent has proved itself. None of this displaces the work your designer does. She keeps doing the graphics, and these give her better material to work with.

How to start

One recommendation, and the cost

I would start with one agent rather than a whole system: the messaging framework and the Outbound Agent, built and handed over in three weeks. It is a contained piece of work, the results are measurable in replies and meetings, and it gives you a clear view of how we work before you commit to anything larger. It is also worth setting the cost against the alternative. Doing outbound properly with a person is a full-time job, and a full-time hire costs in the region of €60,000 a year before employer costs. This is a three-week build for €9,000, and afterwards your own team runs it.
