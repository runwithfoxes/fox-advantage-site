Part one · the thinking

The strategy and plan

Before anything gets built, we decide how Data Intelligence goes to market. Not a positioning document that sits in a drawer, a practical marketing strategy and execution plan: how to reach the market, where the budget goes, and the creative and messaging that carries it. Empirically grounded, and built to do one thing, create fame fast.

Winning early is not about doing a bit more of everything. It is about getting as many inquiries and as much awareness as possible, as quickly as possible, and building the category entry points and mental availability that make Data Intelligence the name people think of the moment their technology costs feel out of control. That means using channels like radio alongside the go-to-market work, chosen on purpose, never incrementally.

What the plan decides
  • How to go to marketThe audiences, the channels (radio and beyond), and the sequence that builds awareness and inquiries fastest.
  • Where the budget goesAllocation across channels and time, geared to fame and mental availability, not vanity metrics.
  • Creative and messagingThe positioning, the argument, the distinctive assets, and the trigger points Data Intelligence owns.
  • How we measure itEmpirically solid: category entry points, mental availability, inquiries and awareness, tracked over time.

An open question · for Data Intelligence

Website Builder

In a world before AI, a new site meant a web designer, a front-end developer and a producer to manage it. The Website Builder is that whole role in one: it takes the brief, does the UX and the UI, builds the pages to your brand, and ships them live to hosting.

You already have a site and a UX/UI designer, so for Data Intelligence this is a different question: how well would AI fit into what you have? If your site is built with AI automation in the middle already, leave it. If not, it may be worth rebuilding it through Claude Code, so your designer can spin up a customised landing page for each client or each proposal with a prompt, no handover. This page you are reading is an example of exactly that. It is an open question I would want to look at with you, not a given.

How it could work
  • Look at what you haveYour site and how your designer works today. If AI is already in the middle of it, we leave it well alone.
  • Decode the designType, spacing, components and imagery become rules Claude Code can build to.
  • Prompt, not handoverA landing page for a named client or a specific proposal becomes a sentence, built and live, no back-and-forth.
  • Your designer, fasterFreed from the repetitive builds to do the work that actually needs a designer.
