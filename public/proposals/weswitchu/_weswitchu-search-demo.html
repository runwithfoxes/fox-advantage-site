People looking for a better energy deal start by searching. Most type it into Google. More and more of them now ask an assistant instead. A search agency runs three things for that: paid search, organic search, and getting named when someone asks an AI. Most of that is daily checking and adjusting against a handful of rules a good marketer sets once. The Search Agent does the daily work, inside your rules.

You have already written a lot of this. There are pages on your site for the cheapest electricity in Ireland, for the best tariff with solar and battery, for EV charging, for heat pumps. The work now is not writing more of them. It is knowing where each one actually lands when somebody searches or asks an assistant, and doing the thing that moves it. That job never finishes, because the questions change, the engines change, and other people keep publishing.

This is not an audit that gets done once and filed. The agent runs every day, inside rules you set at the start, and it keeps working: watching where you are named, noticing when you slip, picking up new questions as people start asking them. It keeps going the same way your own service keeps going for your customers. Drafted answer, human voice

If you have solar, the tariff that suited you before you fitted it is almost certainly the wrong one now. What matters is when you use power rather than how much, so a day and night or a smart tariff usually beats a flat rate once you are exporting during the day. Check which meter type you are on first, because that decides which tariffs you can actually be offered.
