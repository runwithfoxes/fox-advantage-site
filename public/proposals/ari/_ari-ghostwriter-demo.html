Ghostwriter

For an athletic director to recognise ARI's name before they start looking for a system, someone has to publish regularly and publish something worth reading. Historically that has meant hiring a content person, retaining an agency, or working with a freelancer, and none of those options work well here. The people who understand the recruiting cycle are the ones running it, and they do not have time to write. Writers who have time generally do not understand the cycle. ARI holds a great deal of knowledge that athletic directors would find genuinely useful, and very little of it currently reaches them.

Ghostwriter takes ARI's point of view, either from a short voice note or from what ARI already says on its website and in demos, and produces a regular stream of LinkedIn posts and a newsletter written in ARI's voice. The subjects are ones ARI can stand behind: the handover between the coach's prospect and the admissions office's applicant, how to answer a president who asks for funnel data, what a recruiting funnel actually looks like at a Division III college, and the compliance paperwork that nobody in the department wants to own.

The purpose is straightforward. The realistic market is around a thousand colleges, and only a small number of them are actively looking for a system in any given quarter. The buying process usually starts with a trigger: a new athletic director arrives, a president asks for recruiting data, a coach decides to recruit better, or a competitor raises its prices. Consistent content means ARI is already familiar when that moment comes, so ARI is considered rather than having to introduce itself from scratch. The system invents no claims, no statistics and no customers. It publishes ARI's own view, and ARI approves every word before anything goes out.

We have not seen ARI's published writing, so rather than guess at it we wrote a sample ourselves, in ARI's company voice, on the point you agreed with during the call.
