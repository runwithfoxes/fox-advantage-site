Outbound Agent

Outbound has always been a job someone has to do by hand. Someone has to decide which colleges are worth approaching, keep track of what is changing at each of them, find the right person in the athletics staff directory, and then write to that person about the thing that has just changed. Doing that properly across a thousand colleges has traditionally required a full-time hire, an agency, or a freelancer. The Outbound Agent carries out that whole job for you.

ARI already holds the list. There are roughly a thousand Division III and NAIA colleges in the realistic market, and each one publishes a public athletics staff directory, so there is no sourcing phase and no list to buy. The list is also finite, and you only get one chance to make a first approach to each college, so the quality of each message matters more than the volume of messages sent. The agent therefore monitors the full list and only acts when something relevant changes at a particular college: a new athletic director is appointed, a president or VP of enrolment starts asking for recruiting data, a recruiting or athletics role is advertised, or a competitor raises its prices at renewal. The agent then writes to the person concerned about that specific change.

The agent works across two channels, email and LinkedIn. It pauses for your approval at three points: when the watch list is set, when the messages are written, and before anything is sent. Nothing is sent and nothing is spent until you approve it. We would build the agent around ARI's positioning and messaging, hand it over to you, and it would run in your own Claude account.

You give the agent a single instruction, for example "monitor the watch list and write to anyone worth writing to this week". It then monitors the colleges on the list and takes no action until one of the agreed signals appears. The example below shows the signals that came up in a given week and the messages the agent produced from them. The opening of each message is written from that college's own signal, while the closing section, which describes the product, stays consistent across every message.
