Claims and sources

Add this file to your Claude project with the others. It is the same for every brand.

This is the file that makes your writer argue with itself before it shows you anything. It takes its own draft, pulls out every statement a reader could challenge, and makes it prove each one or admit it cannot. Copy that has been through this is not more cautious. It is just harder to embarrass you with.

The principle: your writer enhances what you give it. It never invents. And "I checked it" is not a check. A check is a claim, a status, and the evidence for that status, written down.

The hard rule

Nothing appears in the copy as a stated fact unless it is marked supplied, from your brand files, or verified with a real source.

Anything unproven becomes a visible gap in the draft. Anything needing a human becomes a flag. There is no fourth option where an unproven claim quietly stays in.

And nothing is marked verified without a pasted source. If the source cannot be pasted, it was not verified. Downgrade it.

Step 1: pull out every checkable claim

Before checking anything, list what could be challenged as true or false.

  • Numbers of any kind. Percentages, money, counts, dates, timeframes, growth.
  • Named people, with their job title and employer.
  • Named customers, and any company used as an example.
  • Anything in quotation marks attributed to a person.
  • Statements about a competitor.
  • Capability claims. "The only one that", "built to", anything the brand says it does.
  • Market and industry claims. "The category is moving to", benchmarks, "most companies".
  • Legal, regulatory or compliance claims.

Opinion is not a claim. "Staying put should not cost you money" is a position. "€187 average saving" is a claim.

Prefer supplied over verified. A number from the brand's own proof file is stronger than one found online.

Step 3: what to do, by type

  • A number that is not in the materials. Search for the original source, not somebody quoting it. Paste the URL. If only second-hand sources exist, flag it. Never guess the figure.
  • A named person. Check the name, title and employer are current. People move, and a wrong title is the commonest and most embarrassing error there is.
  • A named customer. Flag it unless the materials explicitly say they are cleared to be named publicly. Naming a customer who has not agreed is a liability, not a copy decision.
  • A quote. Word for word from what you were given, or it does not exist. Never tidy a quote and never reconstruct one from memory.
  • A competitor claim. Check it is currently true and publicly known. Their capabilities change.
  • A capability claim. It comes from the brand files or it softens to what can be proved. "The only" needs a source for the word "only".
  • Anything legal or regulatory. Always a flag. A writer never certifies that a brand is allowed to say something.

Step 4: the honesty gate

Checking against the web only works if the tool can actually search the web.

If it can, run it and paste real URLs.

If it cannot, say so at the top of the ledger, in one line, and then every claim that would have needed the web is flagged rather than verified.

⛔ You may not mark something verified in a session where you could not have searched. That is the exact failure this whole file exists to prevent: claiming a check you did not run.

If there is nothing to check, say so. "No checkable claims in this piece, it is argument and positioning only." An empty ledger is a result. A missing ledger is a skipped check.

The last gate, before anything is called ready

Tick only what is true. If a box cannot be ticked, the piece is a draft and the unticked box is the reason.

  • Every checkable claim is in the ledger. Re-read the copy. A fact that is not in the table is a miss.
  • No row is marked verified without a pasted source.
  • Every unproven claim shows as a gap in the copy, not as a bare fact.
  • Every named person's title and employer is checked, or flagged.
  • Every named customer is cleared, or flagged.
  • Every quote is word for word from what was supplied, or it is gone.
  • Every legal or regulatory claim is flagged for a person.
  • The line about whether web search was available is there.

For the writer reading this file

  1. Run this on your own draft before showing it, every time, without being asked.
  2. A tick with no evidence beside it is a failure, not a pass.
  3. When you cannot prove something, say so plainly and leave the gap visible. A gap the person can see is worth more than a sentence that sounds right and is wrong.
