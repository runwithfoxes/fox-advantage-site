# Kite Insurance: audience

> ⚠️ **Kite is a fictional brand, invented for this course.** Every figure, quote and customer in
> this document is made up. None of it describes a real company, and nothing here should be used as
> research about the insurance market.
>
> Built from an interview, 26 July 2026.

## Who we are for

People who dread the renewal letter and would pay a fair price never to think about it again.

Not the ones who enjoy the haggle. The ones who leave the envelope on the counter for a fortnight
and feel slightly worse every time they walk past it.

That is roughly three quarters of the market, split three ways.

---

## Segment 1: The Payer

**30% of the market.**

Never switches. The letter arrives, the price is up a couple of hundred with no explanation, and
they pay it. They complain about it to somebody that week and then it is done for another year.

**What they are trying to do:** get the year over with.

**When they are in the market:** the renewal date, and only then. There is no other moment.

**Who else is in the decision:** usually one other person in the house, who asks whether we checked
anywhere else, and is told no.

**What they say:** *"It went up again. I don't know why. I didn't do anything."*

**What we say first:** you are overpaying, and you will not have to lift a finger to stop.

---

## Segment 2: The Switcher

**25% of the market.**

Switches most years and saves real money doing it. They are good at this. They also lose a
fortnight of evenings to comparison tabs, hold music and forms that ask for the same information
five times.

**What they are trying to do:** keep the saving without the labour. Their pain is the work, not the
price.

**When they are in the market:** three or four weeks before renewal, deliberately, with a plan.

**Who else is in the decision:** nobody. They own it and they are slightly proud of it.

**What they say:** *"I always switch. I just resent how long it takes."*

**What we say first:** keep the saving, drop the work.

---

## Segment 3: The Juggler

**18% of the market.**

Car and house, and often a second car or a partner's policy as well. Different insurers,
different renewal dates, so something is always coming up. The money matters less than the fact that it never ends.

**What they are trying to do:** stop being the administrator of their own household.

**When they are in the market:** whenever the next one lands, which is several times a year.

**Who else is in the decision:** a partner, and the split is usually that one person does all of it
and is tired of it.

**What they say:** *"There's always one of them due. I couldn't tell you what any of them cost."*

**What we say first:** one place, one date, and it handles all of them.

---

## Who we are not for

27% of the market, turned down on purpose.

**First-timers and young drivers, 11%.** Just bought a first car or moved into a first home. They
have no renewal to dread, so nothing we sell means anything to them yet. Serving them would mean
widening what Kite stands for rather than changing how we say it.

**Cheapest price wins, 9%.** They will move for two euro and no amount of convenience holds them.
Kite is not the cheapest and does not claim to be.

**Commercial and fleet, 7%.** A different product, a different buyer, a different sale.

---

## For the writer reading this file

1. Every piece of copy is aimed at one of the three segments. If you cannot say which, it is aimed
   at nobody.
2. The three share a position and differ in what comes first. Do not write a different Kite for
   each of them.
3. If a draft would land just as well with a first-timer or a price-only shopper, it is too broad.
   Say so rather than softening it.
4. The quotes in this file are invented for the course. Never present them as real customer
   research.
