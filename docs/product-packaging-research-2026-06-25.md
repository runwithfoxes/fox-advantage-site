# How AI companies package and describe products - research

> Compiled 25 June 2026 for the RWF website redesign (storefront / product pages).
> Source: live-site sweep of AI writing tools, AI advertising/creative tools, productized
> consultancies, plus the productized-service naming literature. Every claim traceable to a
> named source below.

---

## The one test for "is this a product?"

The whole field converges on a single forcing function (Jonathan Stark):

> **You can put a price on a card without a discovery call.**

That unpacks into criteria. A slice earns its own card if it passes most of:

1. **One nameable job.** The buyer finishes "I need this to ___" in one clause. Two jobs = two products.
2. **A hard edge.** You can say what it does NOT do. (Designjoy's exclusion list is the move - the boundary is what makes the inside buyable.)
3. **Fixed inputs and outputs.** A brief/assets/data in, a defined deliverable out, no re-scoping per client.
4. **Repeatable.** You've delivered it more than once without rebuilding from scratch (for RWF: a skill already exists).
5. **A time-box.** A stated duration ("in 8 weeks", "48-hour delivery") is the strongest single signal it's a product, not an open relationship.
6. **Distinct from its neighbours.** No "does this overlap with X?" - if two slices share a job + buyer + delivery moment, fold them into tiers.

**Fold vs split rule:** same job, same buyer, same moment → tiers of one product (Standard/Pro). Different job or different buyer → separate cards.

## Naming

**Name by the role or the output. Never by the technology or the capability bucket.**

The names that land in the wild are things the buyer can hold: "Banner Resizer", "Video Ad Generator", "Brand Voice", Every's "Proof / Monologue / Spiral". The ones that need explaining are vendor-named: "Creative Automation", "Unified Editor", "Arc Forge". The word "AI" goes *inside the description*, not in the name (Jasper has nearly dropped "writing" entirely - the buyer assumes AI now).

Winning card structure: **`[output/format] + [maker verb]`** → Ad Maker, Ad Resizer, Brand Voice.

The hierarchy that converts (App Store model - name 30 chars, subtitle 30 chars, description carries the weight):

| Layer | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Card name | Role-noun, specific, ≤4 words | **AI Ad Resizer** |
| Tagline | The job, one line | One brief. Every format. |
| Description open | The outcome | Every campaign lands in every placement, at spec, in your brand |
| Body | Plain nouns of what you get | Format library, crop rules, export pack |

Outcome framing belongs in the *description*, not the *name*. "More Meetings" as a name reads as a promise people disbelieve; as a description line it sells.

## Description formula

**Lead with the verb (what it does), close with the relief (what you get).** Not "a platform that...". Real examples that work:

- "Turn your brand and products into on-brand ads. At scale." (The Brief / Creatopy)
- "Go from generic to great." (Celtra - best four words in the sweep: before-state → after-state, no feature)
- "No Designers. No Guesswork." (AdCreative - naming what *disappears*)
- "Write something great today." (Lex - verb IS the brand promise)

Product-page structure that recurs everywhere: **the job → the problem (what breaks without it, concrete) → what it is (the mechanism) → what you get (plain nouns) → who it's for → price + what happens next.**

Task-named CTAs beat generic ones on a tool ("Resize your ads", not "Get started").

## What the field does, by category

**AI writers.** Nobody sells "AI writing" any more. They sell it by channel/job (Copy.ai: prospecting, ABM, localization) or by team (Claude: Code, Design, Security). "Brand Voice" is now table stakes - Jasper, Writer, Copy.ai all use the exact phrase. Use it; don't invent a synonym. The negative-framing differentiator (Copy.ai: "not dozens of copilots") is underused and strong.

**AI advertising.** The strongest sites name by *output* and chunk by *task*: make vs resize vs test become separate products, and using one pulls you to the next (The Brief's whole rebrand is Discover → Create → Launch → Optimize). Lead descriptions with an imperative verb + the relief ("in seconds", "without a designer").

**Productized consultancies.** Every Studio is the card-discipline north star (one proper-noun name, one-line job, equal real estate, "Try it" not "book a call") - but its architecture is the *inverse* of RWF's: Every builds and KEEPS (hosted, subscription). RWF builds and HANDS OVER. So the closest economic models are **Section** (timeboxed sprints, named tools inside the service - ProfAI, Section HQ) and **Designjoy** (one job, hard edge, price on the page, "ownership transfers on completion"). The "we build it and hand it over" rail is doing work Every never has to do - it should sit on every RWF card.

**Pricing signals product.** Designjoy's price on the page, no call = product. Superside/Smartly "book a demo", no public price = enterprise service. For RWF to read as a productized consultancy, at least the entry tier should carry a public price (range is fine).
