Campaign Manager

Most marketing teams run their tools in parallel: email in one place, LinkedIn in another, ads somewhere else, a spreadsheet for the budget, a separate doc for the brief. The Campaign Manager runs it as one operation. It plans the campaigns, makes the work, runs every channel, and watches the budget. You see all of it in one place and approve what goes out.

The thing that makes it more than a dashboard is Truth: before any campaign starts, your positioning, your messaging framework, your tone, and the levels of metrics that matter are loaded and locked. That is what lets the Campaign Manager propose a real plan, not a template. It grounds every brief in a fact or a hypothesis from your own strategy, or it asks. It does not fabricate a brief. That is the marketer in the machine.

Two gates sit between proposing and sending. You approve the plan before a word is written. You approve the work before anything goes out. Nothing sends or spends without your yes. We build it around your brand, configure it for your channels, and hand it over. You run the approval; it runs everything else.
