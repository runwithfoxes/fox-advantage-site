To cut a short video to a beat, pick the tempo before you make anything, then put every change on a beat. Write "the number lands on beat six" instead of "at about three seconds". Make the music from the same list of beats, and the picture and the sound stay locked together. I tried it on a 15 second reel of Paul's 95:5 rule. Turn the sound on.

The idea comes from a free skill for Claude, [hyperframes-motion-reel](https://github.com/Sunwood-ai-labs/hyperframes-motion-reel-skill), which Sam found on [a list shared on X](https://x.com/Beaver_0x/status/2108619712582279404). It plans a short video beat by beat, builds it as a web page, and writes the music in code from the same plan.

At 120 beats a minute, a beat is half a second, so 15 seconds is 30 beats. Every change goes on one of them. Each row of dots lands on a drum hit, the five lit dots come on one every half beat, the 95 rolls in on beat 11, and the last line lands on beat 23. Because the picture and the sound come from one list, nothing drifts, and if something needs to move, it moves by half a beat.

It cost nothing. There's no AI-made picture in it, only dots, words and sound written in code.

Four things to watch, because ours got them wrong. Keep the picture moving on the beat while people read. In our first cut the dots sat still for two seconds under each line, and Paul said it was timed to the words and lost interest there. Now the five lit dots pulse on every beat while you read.

Get to the point fast. Our dots still take over two seconds to fill in before anything happens, and on a feed that's where people scroll past. Land the number in the first second and a half.

Keep everything in one style. Our first cut ended with the grumpy fox rising in the corner, and Paul said it looked cheap. A soft 3D fox next to flat dots looks like a sticker, so he came out.

Make it work with the sound off. Most feeds play video muted, so most people will never hear the beat. The cuts still land in a steady rhythm, but the words and the dots have to tell the story on their own.

If you want to try it, start with the one number you want people to remember. Give it a beat of its own with a hit under it, and build everything else around that beat.
