## 2025 campaign summary

**Full year, 52 weeks (5 channels):** €308,047 spend → 16,820 quotes started → 5,468 new policies. Blended **CPA €56**, quote→policy conversion **32.5%**.

| Channel | Spend | % spend | Policies | % policies | CPQ | **CPA** | Quote→policy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| price-comparison (switcher) | €19,917 | 6% | 2,028 | 37% | €5.71 | **€9.82** | 58.1% |
| brand-search (all) | €72,775 | 24% | 2,127 | 39% | €10.31 | **€34.21** | 30.1% |
| podcast (juggler) | €36,494 | 12% | 398 | 7% | €28.36 | **€91.69** | 30.9% |
| social (juggler) | €96,320 | 31% | 784 | 14% | €20.99 | **€122.86** | 17.1% |
| radio (payer), 18 wks | €82,541 | 27% | 131 | 2% | €210.03 | **€630.08** | 33.3% |

### What stands out

**1. Spend and results are close to inverted.** Social and radio together took 58% of budget and returned 17% of policies. Price-comparison took 6% of budget and returned 37%. Quarterly CPA was stable (€47–61), so this is a structural allocation issue, not a bad patch.

**2. Social's problem is conversion, not traffic.** It generates the second-highest quote volume at a respectable €21 CPQ, but only 17.1% of those quotes convert vs 30–58% everywhere else — dead steady all 52 weeks. If social quotes converted at even the 30% others manage, its CPA would fall from €123 to ~€70. Worth investigating whether it's landing-page/quote-flow fit or audience quality before cutting.

**3. Radio looks poor even when you credit it generously.** During the two flights (Feb–Mar, Sep–Oct), brand-search quotes ran ~20% above dark weeks at identical spend — a plausible awareness halo. Crediting radio with all of that lift (~101 extra policies) still only brings its CPA from €630 to ~€356, more than double the next-worst channel. Price-comparison and social showed no flight-week lift at all.

**4. Price-comparison hit a ceiling in April–May.** Spend was raised from ~€350 to ~€560/week for 8 weeks. Quotes rose only 10% (61→67/wk): **+€1,656 spend bought ~24 extra policies, an incremental CPA of ~€68** against a €10 baseline. The channel is the most efficient you have, but it does not scale linearly — worth retesting with a smaller step-up before assuming headroom.

### Data caveats — read before quoting these figures

- **An unexplained demand spike, 10–31 March**, drove brand-search quotes +130% and price-comparison +95% with **no change in spend**, then decayed back to baseline. That's roughly **322 policies, ~6% of the year's total, from four weeks nobody paid for.** It sits inside the first radio flight, but the second flight produced nothing like it, so I'd treat it as an external event (competitor exit, PR, pricing change, or an attribution/tracking change) rather than media-driven. Worth confirming with whoever was in-market then — if it's excluded, brand-search CPA rises from €34 to ~€40.
- **Podcast tracking outage, 16–30 June:** three consecutive weeks show normal spend (~€700/wk) but exactly zero quotes and zero policies, bracketed by ~26 quotes/week either side. Almost certainly broken tracking, not a real collapse. It costs podcast ~24 policies and overstates its CPA by ~7%.
- **One exact duplicate row** (2025-07-14, social/juggler) — I removed it; 226 unique rows analysed. No missing values, no other gaps, all 52 weeks present.

### Suggested next steps

1. Confirm what happened in March before it gets baked into 2026 baselines.
2. Fix podcast tracking and restate June.
3. Diagnose the social quote→policy gap — it's the single largest recoverable sum in the plan (~€50k of spend sitting behind a 17% conversion rate).
4. Decide on radio deliberately: it needs a brand/awareness justification, because the acquisition maths doesn't support €82k at €630 CPA.
