## Thread 3: Storm damage refused as wear and tear

adjuster says the felt was already at end of life and the storm just finished it off, so

**thegablewall** (posts: 761)
Normal enough unfortunately, flat roofs are the classic one for this. The policy covers

**thegablewall** (posts: 761)

the decision. If they hold the line you can take it to the ombudsman, its free and they
do overturn these. Takes months though, be warned.

## Thread 4: Flood payout cut because we were underinsured

Started 27 Aug 2025 · 5 posts

**quaywall** (posts: 156)
Hard lesson, sharing it so someone else avoids it. Kitchen flooded in July. Assessor
worked out the damage at €18,600. But the house was insured for €160,000 and their
rebuild figure was €240,000, so they applied something called the average clause and paid
out two thirds. We got €12,400 and found the rest ourselves. Nobody ever mentioned this
clause when we bought the policy.

**Slateface** (posts: 3,204)
Sorry that happened, its brutal and its very common. For anyone reading, the average
clause means if you insure for two thirds of the rebuild cost, they pay two thirds of any
claim. Even a small one. Your sum insured is the rebuild cost, not the market value, and
you need to update it every few years because building costs move.

**BinLidBanjo** (posts: 428)
1 in 6 houses in Ireland is underinsured apparently, read that somewhere last year. No
idea where the number comes from but it would not surprise me at all.

**quaywall** (posts: 156)
It would not surprise me either. Everly never once prompted us to look at the figure in
nine years of renewing, the renewal just rolls over with last years number. I know its our
job to check but still.

**Slateface** (posts: 3,204)
The renewal letter does technically say review your sum insured, in the small print
nobody reads. There are rebuild cost calculators from the surveyors that take ten
minutes. Do it with a coffee, it is the cheapest insurance advice going.

## Thread 8: Apartment insurance, what am I actually meant to buy?

Started 15 Oct 2025 · 4 posts

**fourthfloorflat** (posts: 9)
First apartment, closing next month. The management company says the block is insured and
its in my service charge. So do I need home insurance at all? The bank is asking for
proof of insurance and I dont know what they even want.

**thegablewall** (posts: 761)
Common confusion, you are not being thick. The block policy covers the building, the
structure, usually as far in as the plaster. Everything inside the plaster is yours:
floors, kitchen, your belongings. What you want is contents insurance, and tell the bank
the buildings element is in the block policy, they deal with this daily.

**fourthfloorflat** (posts: 9)
That is the clearest answer I have got from anyone including the broker. So contents only
for an apartment, buildings and contents for a house, generally.

**thegablewall** (posts: 761)
Generally, yes. Duplex and own-door places can be messier, read the lease. But for a
standard apartment in a managed block, contents is the piece thats yours to sort.

## Thread 9: What insurance do I need before mortgage drawdown?

Started 7 Apr 2026 · 4 posts

**keysinmay** (posts: 22)
Solicitor says insurance has to be in place before drawdown and Im only realising I dont
know what that means. Is mortgage protection the same as home insurance? The broker
mentioned life cover aswell and now there are three things and I dont know which are the
same thing.

**Grangemouth** (posts: 1,882)
Three names, two things. Home insurance covers the house itself, fire, floods, and your
stuff. Mortgage protection is life cover that clears the mortgage if you die, and thats
the one people call life cover in this context. The bank wants both before drawdown.
Nobody explains this properly, you learn it in the week you are already drowning in
paperwork.

**keysinmay** (posts: 22)
Thank you. Genuinely could not find a straight answer written down anywhere, every page I
found was trying to sell me a quote before telling me what the thing was.

**Grangemouth** (posts: 1,882)
Thats the category for you. Get the home insurance sum insured from your surveyors
rebuild figure, not the price you paid for the house. Different number, common mistake,
and the wrong one can cost you badly at claim time. See the flood thread on here from
last year.

## Thread 10: Renting a room in a shared house, can I insure my own stuff?

Started 12 Dec 2025 · 3 posts

**boxroombill** (posts: 5)
Renting in a shared house, landlord has the building covered obviously. Tried to get a
quote online for just my own belongings, laptop, bike, camera, and the form would not go
past the do you own this property question. Rang one company and they said they dont
cover renters in shared houses. Is my stuff just uninsurable?

**dampcourse** (posts: 96)
Not uninsurable but its an awkward corner of the market, most of the big names dont want
it. Look for renters contents or tenants insurance specifically rather than home
insurance. A couple of the smaller outfits and some brokers do it. Bike might be cheaper
insured on its own through a cycling policy.

**boxroombill** (posts: 5)

listed. For anyone searching later: the phrase that works is tenants contents insurance,
the normal quote forms just wall you out.
