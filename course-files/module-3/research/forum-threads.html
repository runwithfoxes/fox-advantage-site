Thread 1: Burst pipe in January, still drying out in May

Grangemouth (posts: 1,882) Been through it with a different crowd two years ago, water tank. What worked for me was everything in writing. Ring them if you want but follow every call with an email saying what was agreed. And ask them straight out for a named handler. You are entitled to ask.

Grangemouth (posts: 1,882) Also keep every receipt. Takeaways, launderette, the lot. You can put them all in at the end. We got most of ours back.

TilerNotAPlumber (posts: 214) Update for anyone finding this later. Got a named handler after the email, builder booked for June. Four and a half months start to finish if it holds. The paying out was never the problem, the waiting was.

Thread 3: Storm damage refused as wear and tear

Started 19 Nov 2025 · 5 posts

FlatRoofFiasco (posts: 12) Felt roof on the kitchen extension came up in the October storms. Claim refused. Their adjuster says the felt was already at end of life and the storm just finished it off, so its wear and tear not storm damage. Roof was fine before the storm. €4,100 quote to replace. thegablewall (posts: 761) Normal enough unfortunately, flat roofs are the classic one for this. The policy covers sudden damage not gradual. If you have photos of the roof before, or an old survey saying it was sound, that changes the argument completely. Without paper its your word against the adjusters.

FlatRoofFiasco (posts: 12) House survey from 2022 says roof coverings in serviceable condition. Would that do?

thegablewall (posts: 761) That is exactly what you want. Appeal in writing, attach the survey, ask them to review the decision. If they hold the line you can take it to the ombudsman, its free and they do overturn these. Takes months though, be warned.

Thread 4: Flood payout cut because we were underinsured

Started 27 Aug 2025 · 5 posts

quaywall (posts: 156) Hard lesson, sharing it so someone else avoids it. Kitchen flooded in July. Assessor worked out the damage at €18,600. But the house was insured for €160,000 and their rebuild figure was €240,000, so they applied something called the average clause and paid out two thirds. We got €12,400 and found the rest ourselves. Nobody ever mentioned this clause when we bought the policy.

Slateface (posts: 3,204) Sorry that happened, its brutal and its very common. For anyone reading, the average clause means if you insure for two thirds of the rebuild cost, they pay two thirds of any claim. Even a small one. Your sum insured is the rebuild cost, not the market value, and you need to update it every few years because building costs move.

BinLidBanjo (posts: 428) 1 in 6 houses in Ireland is underinsured apparently, read that somewhere last year. No idea where the number comes from but it would not surprise me at all.

quaywall (posts: 156) It would not surprise me either. Everly never once prompted us to look at the figure in nine years of renewing, the renewal just rolls over with last years number. I know its our job to check but still.

Slateface (posts: 3,204) The renewal letter does technically say review your sum insured, in the small print nobody reads. There are rebuild cost calculators from the surveyors that take ten minutes. Do it with a coffee, it is the cheapest insurance advice going.

Thread 6: Does loyalty count for anything with insurers?

Started 22 Jan 2026 · 4 posts

glenside_row (posts: 1,043) There was a rule change about that a few years back, they are not supposed to price long-standing customers worse than equivalent new ones anymore. Renewals still rise because everything is rising, but the loyalty thing specifically was supposedly dealt with.

Thread 8: Apartment insurance, what am I actually meant to buy?

Started 15 Oct 2025 · 4 posts

fourthfloorflat (posts: 9) First apartment, closing next month. The management company says the block is insured and its in my service charge. So do I need home insurance at all? The bank is asking for proof of insurance and I dont know what they even want.

thegablewall (posts: 761) Common confusion, you are not being thick. The block policy covers the building, the structure, usually as far in as the plaster. Everything inside the plaster is yours: floors, kitchen, your belongings. What you want is contents insurance, and tell the bank the buildings element is in the block policy, they deal with this daily.

fourthfloorflat (posts: 9) That is the clearest answer I have got from anyone including the broker. So contents only for an apartment, buildings and contents for a house, generally.

thegablewall (posts: 761) Generally, yes. Duplex and own-door places can be messier, read the lease. But for a standard apartment in a managed block, contents is the piece thats yours to sort.

Thread 9: What insurance do I need before mortgage drawdown?

Started 7 Apr 2026 · 4 posts

keysinmay (posts: 22) Solicitor says insurance has to be in place before drawdown and Im only realising I dont know what that means. Is mortgage protection the same as home insurance? The broker mentioned life cover aswell and now there are three things and I dont know which are the same thing.

Grangemouth (posts: 1,882) Three names, two things. Home insurance covers the house itself, fire, floods, and your stuff. Mortgage protection is life cover that clears the mortgage if you die, and thats the one people call life cover in this context. The bank wants both before drawdown. Nobody explains this properly, you learn it in the week you are already drowning in paperwork.

keysinmay (posts: 22) Thank you. Genuinely could not find a straight answer written down anywhere, every page I found was trying to sell me a quote before telling me what the thing was.

Grangemouth (posts: 1,882) Thats the category for you. Get the home insurance sum insured from your surveyors rebuild figure, not the price you paid for the house. Different number, common mistake, and the wrong one can cost you badly at claim time. See the flood thread on here from last year.

dampcourse (posts: 96) Not uninsurable but its an awkward corner of the market, most of the big names dont want it.
