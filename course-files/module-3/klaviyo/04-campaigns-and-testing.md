# Campaigns and testing: the one-off sends

Checked against Klaviyo's own pages on 17 Oct 2026. Figures labelled "Klaviyo's figure" are Klaviyo's own, from its own customers, and are attributed revenue (by default the last email opened or clicked in the 5 days before a sale), not revenue the email caused (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding Klaviyo message attribution", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804504250). Klaviyo's email price rises with the number of active profiles and its text price with credits used, so it earns more when lists are bigger and more is sent. That is our reading, not Klaviyo's, and it matters most in the section on how often to send (Source: Klaviyo pricing page, no date shown, checked 17 Oct 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/pricing).

## What is a campaign, and how do I build one?

A campaign is a one-time send to a list or segment that you choose, such as a newsletter, a sale announcement or a product launch. It can go out now or at a time you set. You find it under the Campaigns tab, where you can start from scratch or from a library of ready-made drafts. The wizard has three screens. First you name the campaign, pick the channel and add tags. Then you choose recipients. Then you build the content, from a saved template, the email library, a text-only editor, your own HTML or a blank drag-and-drop page. Last you review and schedule. A campaign needs at least one recipient or it is cancelled automatically, and one campaign can go to as many as 15 lists or segments (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create and send an email campaign", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005054847).

## Who should get it?

Klaviyo's advice is to send mainly to engaged people, not the whole list. Its starting definition is anyone who opened or clicked in the last 30 days plus anyone who subscribed in the last 15 days. If open rates run well above 20%, widen the window to 60 days. If they fall below 20%, narrow it, for example to 14 days (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to increase campaign open rates", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042454031). One catch: Apple Mail can record an open when nobody read the email, and Klaviyo says it cannot tell the two apart, though it adds a True or False "Apple Privacy Open" flag to each open that you can use in segments. So an engaged segment built on opens alone includes some people who never saw the message (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to identify iOS15 Mail Privacy Protection opens", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/4416791883163). File 06 covers this in detail.

Klaviyo's Academy describes a rhythm built on tiers: the most engaged people get the regular weekly and monthly sends, and the full list gets a message only in the biggest sales periods. The Academy's example schedule shows the full list a few times a year (Source: Klaviyo Academy, "Let engagement drive your sending strategy", last updated 14 Jan 2026, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/quick-guides/let-engagement-drive-your-sending-strategy).

The "Don't send to" box removes groups. Klaviyo's own examples are unengaged subscribers, VIPs you have already told, and overlapping audiences. The expected recipient count takes off duplicates, exclusions and unsubscribes before you send (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to exclude lists or segments from a campaign send", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360007016571). A segment of people who ordered in the last 3 days, excluded from promotions, stops you advertising a product to someone who just bought it. If the campaign is scheduled for later, tick "Determine recipients at send time" so the exclusion is checked on the day (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to exclude recent purchasers from a campaign", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/9231956403611). On a paid account you can also describe a segment in plain words inside the wizard and Klaviyo's AI builds it for you to check (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create a segment in the campaign wizard", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/35873493204251).

## When should it send, and what is Smart Send Time?

You pick one of three send types. A fixed time sends to everyone around the same moment. A gradual send goes out in batches, from 1% to 50% of the audience at a time, which spreads website traffic and eases the load on your sending, but it can't be used with an A/B test. Smart send time uses Klaviyo's testing to find the best hour for your audience. You can send at one clock time or in each person's local time, and "Determine recipients at send time" takes a fresh snapshot of the audience just before sending (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding campaign schedule and send options", updated 25 Sep 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050216012).

Smart Send Time is a series of tests, not a switch. It works for email only, and only for campaigns sent to at least 12,000 people. In the exploratory phase each person is assigned a random hour across 24 hours in their own time zone. The number of exploratory sends needed falls with audience size, from 4 to 5 for 12,000 to 17,999 recipients down to 1 above 72,000. The focused phase then sends to the likely best time and to two hours either side, and after that you can send at the best time. Klaviyo says to avoid holidays and time-sensitive messages such as flash sales, to turn Smart Sending off for test emails, to keep your normal sending rhythm, and not to A/B test the same email. The results come in only after the conversion window, which is 5 days by default (Sources: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding Smart Send Time in Klaviyo", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029794371; Klaviyo Academy, "Find the best send time with AI", last updated 6 Oct 2025, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/quick-guides/find-the-best-send-time-with-ai). Klaviyo's results report charts open rate per hour, alongside orders per hour (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding Smart Send Time results", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041411132). Our reading: because Apple's automatic opens count as opens, an audience with many Apple Mail readers should treat the result as a hint to check, not a fact. A brand under 12,000 can still run an A/B send-time test, and Klaviyo suggests an evening slot as a first try (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to A/B test an email campaign", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005228148).

## How often should I send?

This is where Klaviyo's interest and yours differ most, so read its numbers as the vendor's view. Klaviyo's Academy says to send at least one campaign a week, to ramp up slowly over 30 to 60 days if you are sending less than that, and not to jump from one a month to five a week, because sudden jumps look like spam to inbox providers. It says to avoid daily sends to the same people unless a daily series is the point, such as a 30-day challenge people signed up for (Source: Klaviyo Academy, "Let engagement drive your sending strategy", last updated 14 Jan 2026, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/quick-guides/let-engagement-drive-your-sending-strategy).

A Klaviyo course video, from 2024, goes further and says successful brands in an analysis of more than 143,000 Klaviyo brands sent about eight campaigns a month, supported by a "rule of seven" claim that people need to see a brand seven times before buying. No method or source is given for either (Klaviyo's figure, sample not described; Source: Klaviyo Academy, "Getting started with campaigns", last updated 30 Sep 2025, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/courses/getting-started-with-campaigns). The same course suggests mixing three kinds of campaign: useful content once or twice a month, launches and news about twice a month, and promotions weekly. In its own story, a bakery that emailed sales every other day to everyone saw customers get frustrated and margins fall (same course). We hold no outside data on the best send frequency, so there is no independent check on the "at least weekly" line. A practical way to let customers set it is a preference-page question that sorts them into daily, weekly and monthly segments (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create email frequency segments", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000769631).

## How do I A/B test a campaign, and what do I judge it on?

Create the email, click Create A/B test, and Klaviyo copies it into two versions. You can test subject lines, content or send time, and you change one thing only. Klaviyo recommends two variations, and its general testing guide says no more than four. On paid accounts an AI option writes a second subject line from choices such as short against long or emoji against none. The accounts with over 400,000 profiles can also pick a personalised mode where Klaviyo predicts the best version for each remaining recipient (Sources: "How to A/B test an email campaign", as above; Klaviyo Help Center, "Best practices for A/B testing reference", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360045012632).

You choose a winning metric: open rate, click rate or placed order rate. Klaviyo's page suggests open rate for subject line, preview text and sender, click rate for content such as buttons, and placed order rate for content such as best sellers against new items. Our advice for this pack is to judge on clicks and orders, not opens. Klaviyo itself says Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens, that tests may need a higher threshold, and that if you regularly A/B test and more than about 45% of your opens come from Apple Mail, it suggests creating a custom report that includes an MPP property (Source: "How to A/B test an email campaign", as above). In flows Klaviyo goes further and recommends clicks as the winning metric (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to A/B test a flow email", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/6960371049115). File 06 explains why.

The test size and length are set by a slider. In Klaviyo's example 20% of recipients get each version, the winner is chosen after 6 hours and the rest get the winner. Our reading: a 6-hour test can't see orders that arrive over the 5-day attribution window, so a test judged on orders needs a longer window or a 100% send where the winner can still change as data arrives (Source: "How to A/B test an email campaign", as above).

Klaviyo marks a result "statistically significant" when each version has gone to at least 50 people and the win probability is 90% or more. "Promising" is 75% to 89%, and Klaviyo says to rerun it. "Not statistically significant" means a win probability under 75% with at least 1,800 per version. With fewer than 1,800 per version it means a lead of 4% or less and a win probability under 60%. After two or three retests Klaviyo says to move on to a different test (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding statistical significance in Klaviyo campaigns", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052793012). Our reading: 50 people a side is a very small sample, so a small test of a few hundred people is a hint to test again, not proof. If everyone in the test pool is skipped by Smart Sending, the test is cancelled and the campaign must be resent (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding Smart Sending in Klaviyo", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002779311).

## What does good template and email design look like?

Make a few base templates with your logo, colours, header and footer, and reuse them. Klaviyo's Academy lists the same header and footer, a consistent tone, descriptive link text such as "See all offers" instead of "Click here", alt text on every image and strong colour contrast (Source: Klaviyo Academy, "Best practices for email templates", last updated 30 Jul 2025, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/best-practices/best-practices-for-email-templates).

The Help Center's design pages give the detail. The default template width is 600 pixels, and going wider raises the risk of a skewed look on phones (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Optimal email template width reference", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001435352). For full-width images, Klaviyo recommends 1MB or smaller, 600 to 1000 pixels wide and under 2000 tall. PNG usually shows best for images with text, and Outlook and Apple Mail may hide images until the reader allows them, which is why alt text matters (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "About email images best practices", updated 17 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005253688). Columns stack on phones by default, and a campaign's Deliverability tab shows opens by mobile or desktop (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding mobile email optimization", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005254428). Dark mode can turn a dark logo on a transparent background invisible, so use logos that read on both. Klaviyo advises against image-only emails because, without alt text, they can look suspicious to inboxes, and they are hard for screen readers. It cites Litmus that 34% of people viewed email in dark mode in 2022, an old figure (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Dark mode email design best practices", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049181631). The accessibility page adds descriptive links, headers that separate ideas and a logical order (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Accessibility in emails and forms best practice reference", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034711931).

## What do Klaviyo's campaign benchmarks say?

Klaviyo's figure, from its 2026 email benchmark page: campaigns are 94.7% of email sends and flows 5.3%, yet flows bring nearly 41% of email revenue. Flows earn nearly 18 times the revenue per recipient. Flow emails have a 5.58% click rate against 1.69% for campaigns, a 13 times higher placed-order rate, and about 48% of flow revenue comes from new buyers against 16% for campaigns. Average open rates are 31.02% for campaigns (45.12% for the top 10%) and 32.21% for flows (45.8%), and average click rates are 1.69% for campaigns (3.38% top 10%) and 5.58% for flows (10.48%) (Klaviyo's figure; Source: Klaviyo, "2026 email marketing benchmarks by industry", chart dates vary: open rate chart updated 11 Dec 2025, click rate chart 28 Jan 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/products/email-marketing/benchmarks).

Three cautions. First, the email benchmarks page's customer count is blank in the copy we hold. Klaviyo's 2026 abandoned cart report is a different report: it says it analysed more than 110,000 Klaviyo customers, and its figure should not be carried over to the email benchmarks. The first version of this pack said 183,000, which we could not confirm on the page, so treat the sample size as not stated (Source: Klaviyo blog, "2026 Abandoned Cart Benchmark Report", 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks). Second, two charts on that page, headed email revenue per recipient and email order rate, look like text-message charts (our inference from the figures shown), so do not read them as email figures. Third, open rates carry the Apple inflation described above, so the open figures don't tell you what people read.

For a figure to judge your own campaigns by, Klaviyo's Help Center table gives revenue per recipient at the 25th percentile, median and 75th percentile, by yearly revenue and average order value. For a brand with $1M to $5M a year and an average order of $83 to $112, the numbers are $0.14, $0.30 and $0.64 (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Performance benchmarks for email campaigns reference", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033669492). To tell whether email itself earns anything beyond the sales that would have happened anyway, Klaviyo offers holdout groups, but only to accounts with at least 400,000 total profiles, and it suggests running them for 3 months (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Getting started with global holdout groups", updated periodically, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/18138290642971). File 06 covers the wider measurement question.
