# Flows: Klaviyo's automated messages

Checked against Klaviyo's own pages on 17 Oct 2026. Klaviyo's figures in this file are Klaviyo's own, measured on its own customers, and they are attributed revenue: by default the last email opened or clicked in the 5 days before a sale gets the credit, which is not the same as revenue the email caused (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding Klaviyo message attribution", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804504250). Klaviyo's income also grows with the size of the list (email is priced by active profiles, text by credits), so its advice leans towards more profiles and more sending. That is our reading, not Klaviyo's (Source: Klaviyo pricing page, no date shown, checked 17 Oct 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/pricing).

## What is a flow, and how is it different from a campaign?

A flow is a set of automated steps, usually emails or texts, that starts when something happens to a person: they join a list, place an order, start a checkout, or reach a date stored on their profile. A campaign is a one-off send that you build and schedule yourself to a list or segment. Klaviyo's Academy puts it as flows reacting to individual behaviour at the moment it happens, and campaigns going to a pre-chosen group at a time you pick (Source: Klaviyo Academy, "Getting started with flows", last updated 16 Jan 2026, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/courses/getting-started-with-flows). The Help Center gives the same split and the examples: a monthly newsletter or a flash sale is a campaign, a welcome email that goes out when someone signs up is a flow (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create and send an email campaign", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005054847).

Here is a worked example. Say a shop sells a product that people reorder about every 60 days. A reminder that goes to each customer 55 days after their own last order is a flow, because the timing belongs to the person. A Black Friday sale that goes to everyone on one day is a campaign, because the timing belongs to the shop. A subscription renewal works the same way as the reorder reminder, but the clock is a renewal date held on the customer's record, not an order.

## What can start a flow?

Klaviyo lists six triggers. Added to a list, and added to a segment. A metric, meaning any tracked event such as Placed Order or Started Checkout, including events you send in through the API. A date property on a profile. A price drop on something a person viewed or started checkout with. And low stock on the same kind of item. Opened Email and Clicked Email cannot be triggers. A date property can also sit on a custom object tied to a profile, such as a Renewal Date or an Appointment Date (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding flow triggers and filters", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002779051).

## What is the difference between a trigger filter and a flow filter?

A trigger filter looks at the event that starts the flow. If the trigger is Placed Order, a trigger filter can limit the flow to orders that contain a certain product or are over a certain value. Metric-based trigger filters are checked once, when the person enters. A flow filter looks at the person: their properties and what they have done. Klaviyo's newer pages call it a profile filter, and the two names mean the same thing. It is checked when the person enters and again before each email or text, and someone who fails it at one step is skipped for that message but stays in the flow and is checked again at the next step (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding flow triggers and filters", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002779051). An older Klaviyo guide says a segment-triggered flow can never message the same person twice, while the newer page says re-entry settings let you choose once only, every time they re-qualify, or after a minimum gap, so check the setting in the trigger (Sources: same article; "Guide to Flow Triggers and Flow Filters", updated 22 Jul 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002774952).

## Which flows should I set up first?

Klaviyo's own beginner course says to start with three: a welcome series, an abandoned cart flow and a win-back flow, then add more once those are live. The same course walks you through building a fourth, browse abandonment. (Source: Klaviyo Academy, "Getting started with flows", last updated 16 Jan 2026, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/courses/getting-started-with-flows)

Klaviyo's own revenue figures (in the sections below) put abandoned cart and welcome well ahead of the rest. Win-back earns the least per person of the five flows Klaviyo measures, at $0.82 on average (Klaviyo's figure). So on Klaviyo's numbers, welcome and abandoned cart come first. Win-back is there to keep in touch with people who have gone quiet, and its job is different. This is our reading of Klaviyo's two sources side by side. If you don't run an online shop, the cart and browse flows won't fit as they are.

## Welcome series

The trigger is Added to list, usually the main sign-up list. Klaviyo's advice is to keep the email welcome flow separate from the text one, because people can join each channel at different times, and the article says a list-triggered flow runs once per person. New lists are double opt-in by default, so the flow starts only after the person confirms. If you import an old list while the flow is live, everyone imported gets the first message, so set the flow to manual first (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create an email welcome series", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002775172).

Timing: Klaviyo recommends three emails over about a week, the first straight away, the second 3 days later and the third 4 days after that. Its Academy adds a minimum of one day between messages (Sources: same article; Klaviyo Academy, "Getting started with flows", last updated 16 Jan 2026, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/courses/getting-started-with-flows). To avoid discounting people who would have bought anyway, Klaviyo suggests a split that sends the coupon email only to subscribers who have not ordered since joining (same Help Center article).

Klaviyo's figures: the 2026 report on more than 110,000 Klaviyo customers gives welcome email flows an average revenue per recipient of $5.75 and $13.27 for the top 10%, with click rates of 6.5% and 13.3% (Source: Klaviyo blog, "2026 Abandoned Cart Benchmark Report", 2026, no day shown, https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks).

## Abandoned cart and abandoned checkout

Two flows are better than one. Added to Cart catches people who put something in the basket. Started Checkout catches people who entered their details, which Klaviyo treats as stronger intent, so the timing and message can differ. The Added to Cart flow needs the filter "Started Checkout zero times since starting this flow", so nobody gets both. Both need "Placed Order zero times since starting this flow", which stops reminders after a purchase. Klaviyo says many people being skipped for failing that filter is normal and means the flow is working. A "hasn't been in this flow in the last X days" filter stops repeat reminders (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create an abandoned cart flow", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002779411).

Timing: the pre-built flow waits 4 hours. Klaviyo's advice is a first message 2 to 4 hours after checkout starts and a second 20 to 48 hours later. Klaviyo's beginner course recommends 2 to 3 emails in an abandoned cart flow (Source: Klaviyo Academy, "Getting started with flows", last updated 16 Jan 2026, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/courses/getting-started-with-flows). Some store connections sync only hourly, so keep any delay above 1 hour 15 minutes. Content: show the items left behind through the dynamic block, with a button back to the cart, and leave other products out so the message stays on the cart (same article). Klaviyo's report gives one sample sequence: an email at 2 to 4 hours, a text with a discount at about 24 hours, and a last message at 48 hours with alternative products or a feedback request (Source: Klaviyo blog, "2026 Abandoned Cart Benchmark Report", 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks).

Klaviyo's figures from that report: abandoned cart email flows average $6.77 revenue per recipient and $13.70 for the top 10%, the highest of any flow type, with click rates of 6.0% and 11.3%. A split on cart value is Klaviyo's suggested way to offer a bigger incentive only on high-value carts (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding flow branching", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003883992).

## Browse abandonment

The trigger is Viewed Product, which needs Klaviyo's on-site tracking, and it only sees visitors Klaviyo can already identify. Klaviyo wants it to be a lighter touch than a cart reminder. Its filters are Placed Order, Started Checkout and Added to Cart zero times since starting the flow, plus "hasn't been in this flow in the last 30 days", with a delay of at least one hour. Use a static product block here, not the dynamic block the cart flow uses. The flow branching article suggests separate paths for people who viewed products more than 3 times in a chosen period and everyone else; it counts views, not different products. (Sources: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create a browse abandonment flow", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002775252; "Understanding flow branching", as above.) The idea of a separate experience for first-time shoppers comes from the Academy's browse abandonment guide, which splits on whether someone placed an order in the last 365 days, so new customers and lapsed ones get one path and current customers another. (Source: Klaviyo Academy, "Anatomy of a flow: Browse abandonment", last updated 19 Nov 2024, https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/quick-guides/anatomy-of-a-flow-browse-abandonment)

Klaviyo's figures: $2.08 average revenue per recipient, $4.32 for the top 10%, click rates of 5.6% and 9.8% (Source: Klaviyo blog, "2026 Abandoned Cart Benchmark Report", 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks).

## Post-purchase

The trigger is Placed Order. Klaviyo names four types of post-purchase email: transactional, thank you, review request and cross-sell, with an optional instructions email for products that are hard to use. Order and shipping confirmations are transactional and belong in their own flows, with no marketing in them. Splitting first-time from repeat buyers changes the message, and Klaviyo points out that the count includes the order that triggered the flow, so a first-time buyer has placed exactly one (Sources: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create a post-purchase flow", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360028872611; "Understanding flow branching", as above). For products people use up, a replenishment flow is a post-purchase flow with a trigger filter on that product, a reminder timed to the use-up date (about day 25 for a 30-day supply), two reminders and one follow-up with an incentive after the expected date (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create a replenishment flow", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003195232).

Klaviyo's figures: $1.80 average revenue per recipient, $2.77 for the top 10%, click rates of 5.1% and 10.5% (Source: Klaviyo blog, "2026 Abandoned Cart Benchmark Report", 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks). Klaviyo's Help Center also says its research found post-purchase messages get a 217% higher open rate, over 500% higher click rate and 90% higher revenue per recipient than the average campaign. The sample and date are not given, and Apple Mail inflates open rates, so we would not rely on the open figure (Source: "How to create a post-purchase flow", as above).

## Win-back

The trigger is Placed Order, with the filter "Placed Order zero times since starting this flow" and a first delay a little longer than the typical gap between orders for your customers. To find that gap, segment customers with at least two orders in the last two years, export them, and average the "average time between orders" column. Klaviyo suggests three emails: a light first one with 3 to 6 products and an incentive, a second that reminds them of the incentive with an end date, and a last call with a clear unsubscribe link. Products with a set reorder cycle should leave the win-back and get a replenishment flow (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create a winback flow", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002775192).

Klaviyo's own win-back article says that "On average, it is five times more expensive to acquire a new customer than it is to retain existing customers". Harvard Business Review gives a range of five to 25 times, depending on the study and the industry (Source: Amy Gallo, "The Value of Keeping the Right Customers", Harvard Business Review, 29 Oct 2014, https://hbr.org/2014/10/the-value-of-keeping-the-right-customers). James Hurman, introducing a study Klaviyo commissioned, said at a Klaviyo event that he could find no study, research or data behind where the five times line came from (practitioner view, reported second-hand; Source: Australian Marketing Institute, May 2025, https://ami.org.au/knowledge-hub/its-a-big-myth-james-hurman-klaviyo-study-of-anz-online-retailers-1-7m-shoppers-explodes-adage-that-its-5x-cheaper-to-retain-than-acquire-new-customers/).

Klaviyo's figures: $0.82 average revenue per recipient, $1.60 for the top 10%, click rates of 2.8% and 5.8%, the lowest of the five flow types (Source: Klaviyo blog, "2026 Abandoned Cart Benchmark Report", 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks).

## Date-based flows: birthday, reorder, renewal

These use the date property trigger. The date must be stored in a format Klaviyo accepts, such as YYYY-MM-DD, and sign-up forms convert common formats for you. You choose whether messages start on the date or a set time before it, and Klaviyo adds a "target date" step marking the day itself. The flow can repeat monthly, yearly or never. Klaviyo checks all profiles daily, a day ahead, so a flow set live today may miss anyone whose date falls in the next day. If a date changes mid-flow, messages waiting on the old date are skipped (Sources: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to create a date property-triggered flow", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002732652; "Understanding how date-based flows schedule recipients", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054240252).

A birthday flow needs birthdays collected first, through a sign-up form date field, a CSV import, or the store connection. Klaviyo suggests 9am in the recipient's time zone, repeating yearly, and an optional half-birthday flow starting six months before (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to build a birthday flow", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054242492). A reorder reminder can run off a date property or, as above, off Placed Order plus a delay. A renewal reminder can use a custom object date such as Renewal Date (Source: "Understanding flow triggers and filters", as above). We found no Klaviyo benchmark figures for these flows.

## What do the benchmark numbers mean for a small brand?

Klaviyo's Help Center table gives 25th and 75th percentile revenue per recipient by yearly revenue and average order value. For a brand with $1M to $5M a year and an average order of $83 to $112, an abandoned cart flow sits between $2.39 and $5.86, a welcome series between $0.51 and $3.93 and a post-purchase flow between $0.24 and $0.72 (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Benchmarks for flow emails reference", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033669452). The headline $6.77 for carts is an average across more than 110,000 Klaviyo customers of every size, while these ranges are for one size band, so the two aren't measured on the same base. In the same band Klaviyo's campaign median is $0.30 (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Performance benchmarks for email campaigns reference", updated 18 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033669492). Our reading: flows beat campaigns partly because they only go to people who have just shown interest, so the gap shows who is being messaged as much as what the message does.

## How are flows tested and improved?

Test one thing at a time. A single email can have an A/B test on subject line, content or sender, with a winner picked automatically on click rate by default; open rate is the other choice, and Klaviyo recommends clicks because Apple Mail inflates opens. For a flow email, a result counts as significant when at least 10 people across the variations have done the thing measured, more than 500 messages are delivered per variation, one variation has over 90% win probability, and the ranges overlap by under 10%. A date limit ends the test early and just picks the higher rate (Sources: Klaviyo Help Center, "How to A/B test a flow email", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/6960371049115; "Understanding statistical significance in flows", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/11233978755611).

Timing and message count are tested with a random 50% split, a different delay or an extra message on one path, and the same content on both. Don't delete the split while people are waiting on a path (Source: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding what to A/B test in your flows", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054629031). Other steps include time delays and splits of up to 20 paths. A delay goes before a split that depends on something the person does after entering, because a split checks each person once (Sources: "Understanding flow branching", as above; Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding time delays near splits", updated 8 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050334651).

## What is Smart Sending?

Smart Sending skips a marketing message to anyone who got one from you within a set window: 16 hours for email by default, 24 hours for text and push. Skipped messages are not rescheduled. Our reading: a cart reminder can be lost if a campaign went out that morning. Transactional messages are never skipped. Many pre-built flow messages have it on, but check each one (Sources: Klaviyo Help Center, "Understanding Smart Sending in Klaviyo", updated 15 Aug 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002779311; "How to optimize your flow sending frequency", updated 7 Oct 2026, https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/10948996125083).

## Should flows include a discount?

Klaviyo's own cart report warns that discounts can train shoppers to wait. It suggests offering one only on high-value carts. Its Help Center says not to put a coupon in the first cart email, and to hold it for the last email or for people who have never bought (Sources: Klaviyo blog, "2026 Abandoned Cart Benchmark Report", 2026; "How to create an abandoned cart flow", as above). A research paper that the Klaviyo blog calls independent and produced in partnership with Klaviyo, drawing on ProfitPeak's analysis of 176 Australian ecommerce brands (ProfitPeak's data, observational), found 68% of discounted orders came from returning customers, against 11% of full-price orders, and revenue fell 27% on average in the 2 to 4 weeks after a sale. The 176 brands and the three percentages are ProfitPeak's figures, not Klaviyo's. (Source: Klaviyo blog, "Your Discount Strategy Is Making You Poorer", 20 May 2026, https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/discount-pricing-strategies).

The outside experiment points the other way on one thing. Sahni, Zou and Chintagunta ran 70 randomised email promotions on one online ticket resale platform between 2009 and 2011 (outside data). Customers who got offers spent 37% more during the promotion, about $3.03 each, than those who got none. Only about 10% of that rise came through redeemed offers, so about 90% came from people who never used the offer (Sources: Stanford GSB Insights, 21 Jan 2015, https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/navdeep-sahni-email-promotions-prospective-customers-really-do-work; Chicago Booth Review, 11 Jun 2015, https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/just-how-effective-are-free-shipping-emails). Sahni's own caution is that ticketing has few rivals, so shoppers have nowhere else to go. Our reading: the study compared an offer against no email, not an offer against an email without one, so it can't say the discount did the work. It does say that counting only redeemed codes undercounts what an email does, and that the email itself may be doing a lot of the work.
