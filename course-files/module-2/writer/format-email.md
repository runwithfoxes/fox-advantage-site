# How to write an email

Add this file to your Claude project with the others. It is the same for every brand. It describes
the craft of the format, not your brand's voice, which lives in your messaging framework.

Your writer names which framework from this file it is using before it writes, and scores the
finished email against it afterwards.

---

# Instructions for Claude

## Start with what the reader already knows

This governs everything else. Picking the wrong opening for someone's awareness level is the
commonest reason an email fails, and no amount of good writing rescues it.

| They are | What that means | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| **Ready to buy** | Knows the brand, knows the product, has decided | The offer. Nothing else. |
| **Comparing options** | Knows solutions exist, weighing them up | Proof and the difference. |
| **Feeling the problem** | Knows something is wrong, not what fixes it | Name the problem before naming yourself. |
| **Not thinking about it** | Has not framed it as a problem yet | A story, or something that cuts across what they assume. |

Most lists are in the middle two. That is why asking for a meeting in a first email usually fails.
The reader has not arrived at the state where the ask makes sense yet. Earn it first.

## Then pick a shape

Name which one you are using, in the plan, before writing.

| Shape | Use it for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem, agitate, solution** | Someone who already feels the problem. Follow-ups, re-engagement. | The agitate step is where writers go soft. Make the cost specific, do not just say it louder. |
| **Attention, interest, desire, action** | Longer nurture, announcements, content sends. | Too much machinery for a five-line note. |
| **Before, after, bridge** | Where a relationship already exists and they know what you do. | Weak on cold audiences. It assumes they want the after. |
| **Story, insight, action** | Brand-building, longer formats. | The story has to be real and specific. A made-up one is obvious. |
| **Myth, truth, action** | Readers who think they already know this. | Needs real evidence. Invented contrarianism is worse than saying nothing. |
| **Question, reframe, action** | Short emails, surveys, prompting a think. | A lighter version of problem-agitate-solution. |
| **Hook, context, findings, action** | Anything data-led. A report, a survey, a piece of research. | One or two lines of hook, two or three of why it matters and who was asked, three findings, one link. Keep it to 200 words. |

## Length

Default to shorter. Under about 125 words in the body, and under 75 if you are writing to someone
senior.

Go longer only when the click is not the point, for instance an invitation carrying real details,
or a piece making an argument the reader should finish with rather than click into. Longer is a
decision, not the fallback.

If there is time, write both and let them test it.

## How personal it sounds

Pick this before writing, because the sign-off sets the voice more than anything else does.

| | Sounds like | Sign off with |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate** | The brand, "we" | The team, or the company |
| **A team** | A defined group, still "we" | The named team |
| **A named person, speaking for the team** | "We", but somebody carries it | A person. Their title only if the brand actually uses one |
| **A person, lightly** | Measured "I" | A person, title, sometimes a photo |
| **A person, fully** | First person throughout | Just their name |

Most outbound sits in the first three. Save the last two for genuine one-to-one moments. A cold
first email written as a personal note reads as performance, not warmth.

⛔ **Never invent a job title to fill the sign-off.** If the brand signs with a first name, sign with
the first name. A made-up title is a fabricated fact about a real company, and it is the kind that
survives into a hundred emails because nobody thinks to check the signature.

⭐ **Name the register in the plan, before writing, and say who signs.** It is the decision the rest
of the email hangs off. Get it wrong and the piece can be the right shape, one idea and the right
length while not reading as an email at all: no greeting, nobody addressed, no reason it arrived
today, nothing to do at the end.

## Subject lines

- Sentence case. Title Case Reads As Template.
- Say the thing. A subject that hides the point to earn the open annoys the reader who opens it.
- Specific beats clever. A date, a number or a name will usually outperform a pun.
- If it makes a promise, the first line has to pay it.

## Sequences, if there is more than one

**A sequence is not a set of reminders.** One short email cannot make a whole case, so the case is
distributed across four or five, and each one carries a different part of it. A follow-up is the
next chapter, not a nudge.

| Touch | Roughly when | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 | Show you understand their situation. One specific observation. |
| 2 | +3 days | A different angle and a new proof point. Never "just following up". |
| 3 | +7 days | Evidence. Something outside the brand's own voice. |
| 4 | +10 to 14 | The direct ask, with one new reason to act. |
| 5 | +17 | An easy way out. Permission to say no costs nothing and gets replies. |

Rules of thumb worth knowing: the first follow-up alone adds a large share of the replies, three to
four follow-ups is the sweet spot, and roughly four in ten replies come from the follow-ups rather
than the first email. Space them three or four days apart early, wider later.

⛔ **Never repeat an argument in new words.** The benefit appears every time. The framing changes.
If somebody could read touch three and learn nothing they did not get in touch one, the sequence
has failed.

Shorten it when the relationship is warmer. Someone who met you at an event needs three touches,
not five, and the meeting can be asked for in the first.

## Before you show it

- Which awareness level, which shape, which register. All three named in the plan.
- The subject line and the first line agree with each other.
- One idea. If there are two, there are two emails.
- Slop rules run, both passes.
- Claims ledger shown.
- Scored against the messaging framework, honestly.
