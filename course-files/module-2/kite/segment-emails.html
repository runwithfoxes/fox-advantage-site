1. The Payer

Pillar: not punished for staying. Buying situation: the renewal letter lands.

Subject: Your renewal is up €200

``` Hi Sarah,

Your renewal landed this week and it's up €200, which would make sense if something had happened. Nothing did. Same car, same house, no claim in between.

So what changed? Did the Civic join Formula 1?

I'd love to tell you that's unusual. It isn't. Staying where you are is the thing you get charged for.

We work the other way round. Before your renewal comes up we quote the market for you, and if there's a better price we move you to it. The first time we did that for people last year they were €187 better off on average.

Five minutes to set up and then you never think about it again. Have a look whenever you get a chance.

Aoife Kite ```

2. The Switcher

Pillar: it renews itself. Buying situation: three weeks out, planning.

Subject: The bit nobody counts

``` Hi John,

You switch every year and you're good at it, so you're a couple of hundred better off than the people who don't.

The bit nobody counts is what it costs you to get there. Two evenings, eleven browser tabs, a phone call where you read out your own address twice, and a file on your desktop called FINAL QUOTE 4.

What are you, a procurement department?

That's the part we take off you. We quote the market before your renewal and move you if we find better, and 94% of the time we don't need anything from you at all.

Same saving, none of the evenings. Worth a look.

Aoife Kite ```

3. The Juggler

Pillar: not punished for staying. Buying situation: whenever the next one lands.

Subject: March, June and October

``` Hi Sarah,

Your car renews in March, the house in June and the second car some time in October. Three companies, three logins, and three letters a year that all say more or less the same thing.

Somewhere in there it stopped being insurance and turned into a standing admin job. What are you, a renewal calendar?

We put them on one date instead, and before anything renews we quote the market for all of them. So far no year has cost our customers more than staying put would have.

One renewal a year, and a note telling you what we found.

Aoife Kite ```

How these landed on email, and what it cost to get here

Started as social posts and moved to email on Paul's call: "This entire thing is a copywriter." Three corrections got them there, and each one is a rule worth keeping.

⛔ Stacked fragments read as abrasive. The social version ran on clipped one-liners. In an email that reads as somebody snapping at you. Same jokes, same specifics, sentences with air around them.

⛔ An email is written by a person to a person. It opens on a name and it closes on a name. Aoife is invented, like everything else here.

⛔ The strapline and the button were the tell. "Kite. It shops around. You don't." and a [Get a quote] button turned it back into an ad. Paul: "It's not an ad." Nobody signs off an email to a person with a brand line. The endline stays in the brand's kit for ads, and stays out of correspondence.

⚠️ Knock-on nobody has actioned: the AI Writer agent card was briefed as three social posts, truncated in a left list. These are emails with subject lines, so the card's archetype may need to change or it needs the social versions back. Raise it before building.

What the drafting taught, worth keeping

⭐ The device is "What are you, a ___?" It carries the disbelief in one line, it repeats across all three without wearing out, and it is the thing that makes this a voice rather than three one-off jokes. Use it sparingly and never twice in one piece.

⛔ The tone spec's "the customer speaks, Kite arrives at the end" is a radio convention. Read cold in writing, first person is nobody. Written work is second person, the brand talking to you.
