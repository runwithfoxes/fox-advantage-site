# Kite Insurance: proof

> ⚠️ **Kite is a fictional brand, invented for this course.** Every number, quote and claim below
> was made up to make the example work. None of it is real, and none of it should be repeated
> anywhere as a fact about insurance.
>
> Built from an interview, 26 July 2026.

## What we can put a number on

Each of these has a source, because a number without one is not proof.

- **Average saving the first time we shop around for you: €187.** All policies renewed in 2025, measured
  against what the customer paid the year before. *Source: internal renewal data, Jan 2026.*
- **94% of renewals complete with no customer input.** The other 6% need a change the customer has
  to confirm, usually a new address or a new car. *Source: policy system, full year 2025.*
- **First claim response in under an hour, average across the year.** *Source: claims log, 2025.*
- **Average claim settled in 4 days.** Category average is closer to three weeks. *Source: claims
  log, 2025. [unverified] the category comparison is an industry estimate we have not audited, so
  do not use it as a hard number in copy.*
- **41,000 policies, opened since 2022.** *Source: month-end policy count, Dec 2025.*

## What we can do that others cannot

**The quoting and the claims admin are done by agents, not by a call centre.** That is where the
cost sits in this business, so our cost per policy is lower and the difference goes to the price.

**It is hard to copy because of what it would cost them to stop.** Ardline has buildings and staff
and a way of working built around a phone queue. They can put a chatbot on the front of it. They
cannot take the call centre out of the middle of it without dismantling the company. That is the
moat, and it is temporary, which is worth being honest about internally.

## Track record

Trading since 2022. Four full renewal cycles. No year in which shopping around produced a
higher premium than staying put, across the book as a whole.

## What customers say

Invented for the course, and never to be presented as research.

- *"I didn't do anything. It just told me it had moved me and what I'd saved."*
- *"I used to put a day aside for this."*
- *"I still don't fully trust it, so I checked. It was right."*

That third one is the most useful line in the document. It is what the doubt actually sounds like,
and copy that pretends the doubt is not there will not land.

## What we cannot prove yet

Listed so nobody claims it by accident.

- **Whether the saving holds after year one.** The category is full of cheap first years and quiet
  creep afterwards. We believe ours holds because we shop around every year, but four cycles on a
  young book is not proof.
- **Satisfaction at claim time at scale.** The claims numbers are good and the volume is small.
- **That people stay.** Retention looks strong and the book is too young for that to mean much.

## For the writer reading this file

1. Every claim in a piece of copy traces to a line in this document. If it does not, it does not
   go in.
2. Never use a number without the thing that makes it true. €187 on its own is a boast. €187 saved by
   shopping around for you, measured against last year's price, is proof.
3. Anything marked `[unverified]` stays out of customer-facing copy.
4. Do not write around the doubt. Segment 1 in particular half expects a catch, and the honest
   version reads better than the confident one.
