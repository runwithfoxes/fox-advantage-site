Kite Insurance: positioning statement

⚠️ Kite is a fictional brand, invented for this course. The numbers inside the statement come from Kite's proof document and are made up too.

Built from an interview, 26 July 2026. Feeds on audience.md, competitors.md and proof.md.

The statement

For people who dread the renewal letter, Kite is the car and home insurance that renews itself, because it shops around for you every year and saves €187 on average.

The four slots, and where each one came from

Audience. People who dread the renewal letter. From audience.md. It excludes the first-timer, the price-only shopper and the commercial buyer, which is 27% of the market, on purpose.

Frame. Car and home insurance. Kept deliberately ordinary, and narrow enough to be true. See the rejected version below for why the clever frame lost.

Difference. It renews itself. One idea, and no competitor can say it: Ardline, Snapcover and Everly all compete to win the moment the letter arrives. None of them removes the letter.

Proof. The €187 average saving, from proof.md.

⭐ This slot was wrong in the first draft and the fix is the most useful thing in this document. It originally read "completes 94% of renewals without you touching it", which proves the convenience and nothing else. Paul's objection, and it is the objection any customer makes: "I am only interested in an insurance company renewing itself if I believe it's got me the best price." Without the money, an insurance policy that renews itself is a party trick, and a suspicious one.

So the proof carries the saving, and 94% moves into the copy where it does its real job on segment 2, the people whose pain is the labour rather than the price.

What the saving does not claim. Not the cheapest in the market. Kite turned that segment down and would lose that fight to Snapcover anyway. It claims you are not being punished for staying, which is what shopping around actually buys you.

What we rejected, and why

Kept on purpose. Knowing what was turned down is worth as much later as the answer.

Version B, framed as a renewal service. "For people who dread the renewal letter, Kite is the renewal service that…" Rejected. Nobody has a mental shelf for a renewal service, so nobody goes looking for one. Positioning to a frame the customer does not shop in is not positioning, it is being invisible. The chore is what we remove, not the category we compete in.

Version C, led on price. "…the insurance that costs less because the admin is automated." Rejected. That is Snapcover's fight and we lose it the year somebody undercuts us. It also makes the cheapest-price segment our audience, and we turned that segment down two documents ago.

The decision

Chosen by the founder, 26 July 2026, in her words:

"B is what we are, but it is not a thing anyone searches for. C wins this year and loses the next one. A is the only one where the thing we built is the thing we say."
