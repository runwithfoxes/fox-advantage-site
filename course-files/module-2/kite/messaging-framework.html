Kite Insurance: messaging framework

⚠️ Kite is a fictional brand, invented for this course. Every number and quote is made up.

Built from an interview, 26 July 2026. Feeds on positioning-statement.md, which feeds on audience.md, competitors.md and proof.md. Nothing here may contradict the statement.

The value proposition, in one line

Insurance that renews itself, and shops around for you before it does.

What it does, and what that means

What it does. Every year, before the renewal date, Kite quotes the market for you, moves you if there is a better price, and completes the paperwork. You get told what it found and what it chose.

What it means. The fortnight disappears, and so does the quiet penalty for staying put. Two different things, and different people care about each one.

The three pillars

1. It renews itself. The difference. Nobody else removes the letter.

2. You stop being punished for staying. The loyalty penalty is the category's oldest habit. Kite shops around every year on your behalf, which is the only way staying and saving can both be true.

3. It shows you what it did. Every automatic renewal comes with what was found and what was chosen. This pillar exists because the doubt is real and copy that ignores it sounds like a catch.

The buying situation for each column. Segment 1 is only in the market on the renewal date. Segment 2 is in the market three or four weeks before it, deliberately. Segment 3 is in the market several times a year, whenever the next one lands.

Proof under each pillar

  • It renews itself. 94% of renewals complete with no customer input. Source: policy system, 2025.
  • Not punished for staying. €187 average saving the first time we shop around, measured against last year's price. Source: internal renewal data, Jan 2026. And no year in which shopping around produced a higher premium than staying put.
  • It shows you what it did. Every automatic renewal sends what was found and what was chosen. Capability, not a number.

What we will never say

  • Anything on the entry-fee list in competitors.md: trusted, here when you need us, we pay out, a quote in two minutes, great service.
  • Cheapest. Kite is not, and claiming it starts a fight with Snapcover we lose the year somebody undercuts us.
  • Anything that hides the automation. The doubt is real. Copy that talks around it reads as a catch, and pillar 3 exists to meet it head on.
  • Any claim marked [unverified] in proof.md, including the three-week category comparison.

For the writer reading this file

  1. Every piece of copy is one pillar, aimed at one segment, in one buying situation. Name all three before drafting. If you cannot, the brief is not ready.
  2. A pillar without its proof is a slogan. Carry the number.
  3. Do not write a fourth pillar. If a draft needs one, bring it back rather than inventing it.
